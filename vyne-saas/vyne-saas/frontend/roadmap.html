📋 Executive narrative
This insurance carrier faces a defining transformation challenge: legacy infrastructure blocks the real-time, AI-driven capabilities required to compete in a market reshaped by climate volatility, cyber risk, and customer expectations for personalization. The gap analysis reveals no initiatives ready for immediate launch—foundational weaknesses across data, technology, processes, governance, and specialized talent create a web of dependencies that must be resolved systematically. The roadmap structures a realistic 36-month journey organized around three principles: foundation before initiatives, shared investments before siloed efforts, and honest sequencing that respects capital cycles, talent scarcity, and regulatory realities. **Phase 1 (Months 1-9): Foundation** establishes the enterprise AI platform that both strategic initiatives require. With data maturity at 2.5 and technology infrastructure at 2.2—well below the thresholds needed for actuarial pricing or claims automation—the organization must build before it can deploy. The dual-track architecture investment addresses the diagnostic finding that legacy batch systems block all personalization and dynamic pricing; rather than attempting risky rip-and-replace, the approach layers modern API-enabled infrastructure atop existing systems to enable real-time AI while preserving operational continuity. Climate and cyber data remediation launches immediately, driven by regulatory filing deadlines and the finding that data incompleteness creates immediate compliance risk. Process redesign introduces the federated AI delivery model—embedding data scientists directly in claims and underwriting rather than relying on the overstretched central team identified in diagnostics—breaking the deployment bottleneck that currently extends cycles to multiple weeks.
